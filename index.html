Inside the house

The details are the whole difference.

Small things decide whether a person with dementia keeps their independence or slowly loses it. These are the ones we got right first.

A room, not a bed

Private rooms with real furniture, daylight on two sides, and space for the things your mother already owns. Familiar objects are what a person with memory loss holds on to.

A bathroom that does not fight back

Level-entry roll-in shower, grab bars at the toilet and along the wall, a handheld head and a seat. Most falls in care happen in the bathroom, so this is the room we rebuilt first.

Outside, safely

A wide covered deck and a fully fenced garden with raised beds the residents plant themselves. Being outdoors without being supervised at every step is its own kind of dignity.

Life enrichment

Montessori memory care, in plain terms.

We build the day around what a resident can still do, never around what they have lost.

1

Something to do together

Baking, folding laundry, planting the raised beds. Sunday tea, Thursday happy hour, birthdays, and family dinners every quarter.

2

Something to keep them moving

For someone who wants it, setting a table or carrying linens does more for balance than an exercise class, and it does not feel like therapy. Outings to the senior center when they want them.

3

Something worth thinking about

Puzzles and music chosen for the person, at a pace that will not defeat them. Pet therapy, visits from children, and church groups for anyone who wants them.

How Montessori care works here, day to day

Mealtimes

Fresh meals, cooked in our kitchen.

Three meals and two snacks a day, from a six-week rotating menu reviewed by a registered dietitian. Special diets are made exactly as your doctor orders. Everything is cooked for our residents, and anyone who enjoys being in the kitchen is welcome to join in, though nobody is ever expected to.

Is this the right home

Who we are a good fit for.

We are likely a good fit if

  • Your mother or father is living with dementia, Alzheimer's or another memory condition.
  • They need help through the night, not only during the day.
  • Evenings are hard: confusion, wandering, agitation.
  • A large building has already said no, or you worry it would.
  • They also live with a mental health diagnosis alongside memory loss.
  • They are coming home from hospital and cannot yet be alone.
  • You are paying privately or with long-term care insurance.

We are probably not the right home if

  • You need a Medicaid bed today. We are private pay only at the moment.
  • Your parent needs hospital-level or skilled nursing care around the clock.
  • You are looking for an apartment with independent living and minimal support.
  • Care needs have gone beyond what six residents and our staffing can safely cover.

If we are not the right home, tell us anyway. We know the other homes in Thurston County and we will point you somewhere honest.

Questions families ask

The things people want to know.

How is an adult family home different from assisted living?

An adult family home is a licensed private residence caring for a small number of people, six in our case, rather than a building with dozens of rooms. Washington caps every adult family home at six residents. It means far more one-to-one attention, and it feels like a house, because it is one.

Do you specialize in dementia care?

Yes. Olympia Senior Care was built specifically for people living with dementia, Alzheimer's and other cognitive conditions. Peter, the owner, is a board-certified psychiatric nurse practitioner who has worked with memory loss since 2008, and our caregivers are trained in Montessori methods for dementia before they ever work a shift. The home is licensed by DSHS with both the Dementia and the Mental Health specialty designations.

How much does it cost?

Care costs depend on your loved one’s room, care needs and level of support.

Our private-suite rates begin at $9,000 a month, with care services explained clearly during your consultation.

We will walk your family through what’s included before you make a decision.

That covers the room, all meals, utilities, housekeeping, laundry, activities, our health maintenance program, nursing oversight and all the personal care in the care plan.

  • No move-in fee.
  • No administrative fee.
  • No move-out cleaning fee.
  • Furniture, if you need it, at no cost.

What payment methods do you accept?

We accept private pay and long-term care insurance. At this time we are only taking private-pay residents, so we are not able to accept Medicaid.

How many caregivers are on duty?

We staff one caregiver to every three residents, and someone is awake and on duty twenty-four hours a day, including overnight. Nobody here waits for help.

My mother wanders and gets agitated in the evening. Can you handle that?

Yes, and it is worth saying plainly, because it is the reason a lot of families are turned away elsewhere. Sundowning, wandering and agitation are dementia, not misbehavior. The house is single-story with clear visual cues so someone can move around safely on their own, and the day is built so that late afternoon has something calm and familiar in it rather than nothing. Come and talk to us about what your mother actually does, and we will tell you honestly whether we are the right home.

Can we visit before deciding?

Please do, and please come more than once if it helps. Tours are by appointment, seven days a week, and there is no script. Ask us anything, meet the caregivers, and stay as long as you like.

What happens as my parent's needs increase?

Care plans here are written for the person and updated as things change, with a psychiatric nurse practitioner involved in that decision. We also support residents alongside hospice, so families are not forced to move someone again at the hardest possible moment.

What areas do you serve?

We are in West Olympia, and we welcome families from Olympia, Lacey, Tumwater and across Thurston County.
